### Key Visitor Facts
- 54 galleries open to the public across 22 collections; ~7 km of exhibition route; ~4.8 km standard visitor path.
- ~20,000 works displayed (from a total collection of ~70,000); 6.8 million visitors in 2024 (world's #2 art museum after the Louvre).

## 2. THE STANDARD VISITOR ROUTE (IN ORDER)

The museums are a series of interconnected wings wrapping around the Cortile del Belvedere. The main route runs roughly north→south, designed to culminate at the Sistine Chapel.

1. **Entrance** (Viale Vaticano) — ticketing, security, cloakroom
2. **Atrium / Cortile delle Corazze** (Courtyard of the Cuirass) and **Atrium of the Four Gates (Quattro Cancelli)**
3. **Cortile della Pigna** (Pinecone Courtyard) — open-air courtyard with the bronze Pinecone and Pomodoro's Sphere
4. **Museo Gregoriano Egiziano** (Gregorian Egyptian Museum) — Rooms I–IX + Hemicycle + Terrace of the Niche
5. **Museo Chiaramonti** — long loggia with ~1,000 sculptures
6. **Braccio Nuovo** (New Wing) — 68m neoclassical gallery
7. **Museo Pio-Clementino** — Gallery of the Candelabra, Greek Cross Gallery, Octagonal Court, Sala Rotonda, Gallery of the Statues, Gallery of the Busts, Cabinet of the Masks, Sala delle Muse, Sala degli Animali
8. *(Escalator/stairs to upper floor)*
9. **Museo Gregoriano Etrusco** (Gregorian Etruscan Museum) — 22 rooms
10. **Galleria dei Candelabri** (Gallery of the Candelabra) — ~80m corridor, six sections
11. **Galleria degli Arazzi** (Gallery of the Tapestries) — ~75m corridor
12. **Galleria delle Carte Geografiche** (Gallery of Maps) — 120m corridor
13. **Sala dell'Immacolata Concezione** (Room of the Immaculate Conception)
14. **Stanze di Raffaello** (Raphael Rooms) — Sala di Costantino → Stanza di Eliodoro → Stanza della Segnatura → Stanza dell'Incendio del Borgo
15. *(Descent via stairs/corridor)*
16. **Cappella Sistina** (Sistine Chapel) — ceiling, wall frescoes, Last Judgment
17. **Exit via Bramante (Momo) Staircase** — double-helix spiral, back to Viale Vaticano

**Side branches off the main route:**
- **Pinacoteca Vaticana** — separate wing near the entrance; best visited at the start or as a detour after the Sistine Chapel
- **Appartamento Borgia** (Borgia Apartment) + **Collezione d'Arte Moderna e Contemporanea** — on the way toward the Sistine Chapel
- **Sala delle Nozze Aldobrandine** (Room of the Aldobrandini Wedding)
- **Museo Cristiano**, **Musei della Libreria Vaticana**, **Museo Profano**, **Museo Gregoriano Profano**, **Museo Pio Cristiano** — ground floor
- **Basement collections:** Museo Filatelico e Numismatico (Philately & Numismatic), Museo Etnologico "Anima Mundi" (Ethnological), Padiglione delle Carrozze (Papal Carriages)

## 3. MUSEO PIO-CLEMENTINO (Classical Sculpture)

Founded 1771 by Clement XIV; enlarged by Pius VI. The first public museum of antiquity in the modern sense. 12 rooms/galleries including the Octagonal Court.

### 3.1 Octagonal Court (Cortile Ottagono / Cortile delle Statue)
The fulcrum of the museum; the first nucleus of the papal collection of classical sculpture assembled by Julius II (1503–1513). Several sculptures remain in their early-16th-century positions.

| Artwork | Artist | Date | Medium/Dimensions | Description | Location |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Laocoön and His Sons** (Laocoonte e i suoi figli) | Agesander, Athenodoros & Polydorus of Rhodes | c. 40–20 BCE (Hellenistic) | Marble, h. 208 cm | Trojan priest Laocoön and his sons Antiphantes and Thymbraea are strangled by sea serpents sent by Athena/Poseidon after he warned against the Trojan Horse. Discovered on the Esquiline Hill in 1506; purchased by Julius II. Pliny called it "superior to all other works of painting and sculpture." The defining masterpiece of Hellenistic pathos and anatomy. | Octagonal Court |
| **Apollo Belvedere** (Apollo del Belvedere) | Leochares (Roman copy) | c. 120–140 CE (copy of 4th c. BCE Greek original) | Marble, h. 224 cm | The god after shooting his arrow, in a pose of ideal grace and contrapposto. The benchmark of classical male beauty for 500 years; endlessly copied and quoted (by Canova, Thorvaldsen, etc.). Found in Rome in the late 15th century; documented at the Belvedere by 1509. | Octagonal Court |
| **Belvedere Torso** (Torso del Belvedere) | Apollonius of Athens (signed) | 1st century BCE (copy of early 2nd c. BCE bronze) | Marble, h. 1.59 m (Inv. 1192) | A powerfully seated male nude, muscles twisted in a spiral. Now identified most plausibly as Ajax the Lesser contemplating suicide. Michelangelo revered it above all antiquities and used it as the model for many Sistine Chapel figures. In the Vatican collections between 1530–1536. | Sala delle Muse (Hall of the Muses) |
| **Perseus Triumphant** (Perseo trionfante) | Antonio Canova | 1804–1806 | Marble, h. 228 cm | Perseus holds the severed head of Medusa, standing in graceful contrapposto. Canova's masterpiece created after the Napoleonic looting; symbol of the restored collection. | Sala del Perseus (dedicated room) |
| **Biga (Chariot)** | Roman | 1st–2nd century CE | Marble | Ancient two-horse chariot sculpture displayed in the Sala a Croce Greca / Octagonal Court area. | Octagonal Court |

### 3.2 Sala Rotonda (Round Hall)
Shaped as a miniature Pantheon; ancient floor mosaics; perimeter statues.

| Artwork | Description |
|---|---|
| **Gilded Bronze Hercules** | Colossal bronze statue of Hercules, found near the Theatre of Pompey; a rare surviving monumental ancient bronze. |
| **Braschi Antinous** | Over-life-size marble bust of Antinous (Hadrian's beloved), found at Palestrina; one of the finest Antinous portraits. |

### 3.3 Greek Cross Gallery (Sala a Croce Greca)
| Artwork | Description |
|---|---|
| **Porphyry Sarcophagus of St. Helena** | Sarcophagus of Helena, mother of Constantine, made of imperial porphyry (red Egyptian stone reserved for the imperial family). |
| **Sarcophagus of Constance** | Porphyry sarcophagus of Constantina (Constance), daughter of Constantine. |

### 3.4 Gallery of the Statues (Galleria delle Statue)
| Artwork | Description |
|---|---|
| **Sleeping Ariadne** (Arianna) | Roman copy of a 2nd-century BCE original; Ariadne abandoned on Naxos, discovered in the 16th century and long mistaken for Cleopatra. A favorite of Renaissance and Baroque artists. |
| **Bust of Menander** | Portrait bust of the Greek New Comedy playwright Menander. |
| **Barberini Candelabra** | Ancient marble candelabrum. |

### 3.5 Gallery of the Busts (Galleria dei Busti)
Large collection of ancient Roman portrait busts; the red marble papal throne formerly in the Basilica of St. John Lateran.

### 3.6 Cabinet of the Masks (Gabinetto delle Maschere)
Floor mosaic with ancient theater masks from Hadrian's Villa. Statues displayed along the walls, including **The Three Graces** (a version of the famous Sienese/Capitoline group).

### 3.7 Sala delle Muse (Hall of the Muses)
Opened to the public in 1784; displays sculptures from the Villa of Cassius near Tivoli (Hadrianic period) — Apollo with lyre, the Nine Muses, Athena, herms, portraits. Ceiling frescoes by Tommaso Conca (Apollo and the Muses). The **Belvedere Torso** is the centerpiece (see above).

### 3.8 Sala degli Animali (Hall of the Animals)
Named for its many ancient animal statues: the **Giaguaro (Jaguar)**, **Meleagro (Meleager)**, river-god statues including **Divinità fluviale (Arno)** — reclining figures of the Arno and Nile rivers.

### 3.9 Additional Pio-Clementino highlights
- **Apoxyomenos** (Scraper) — Roman copy of Lysippus' famous athlete scraping oil from his body.
- **Hermes Belvedere** — Roman copy of a Greek original.
- **Ercole Mastai** — statue associated with the Mastai family (Pius IX).
- **Sarcofago di Scipione Barbato** — epitaph of Lucius Cornelius Scipio Barbatus, important early Latin inscription.

## 4. MUSEO CHIARAMONTI & BRACCIO NUOVO

Founded by Pius VII Chiaramonti; arranged by Antonio Canova (1806–1807). A long loggia of ~1,000 sculptures — one of the most important collections of Roman portrait busts. After Napoleon's looting (Treaty of Tolentino 1797) and the 1815 recovery (Congress of Vienna, Canova's diplomacy), the museum was rebuilt.

### Highlights
| Artwork | Location | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Heracles with infant Telephos** | Chiaramonti loggia | Roman copy of a Greek original. |
| **~1,000 Roman busts** | Chiaramonti loggia (both walls, ~300 m) | Emperors, emperors' wives, philosophers, orators — a "gallery of famous names from antiquity." |
| **Augustus of Prima Porta** (Augusto di Prima Porta) | Braccio Nuovo | 1st century CE marble statue, h. ~2.03 m. Found 1863 at the Villa of Livia at Prima Porta. Augustus in military dress (adlocutio pose), cuirass decorated with allegorical reliefs (Parthian returning standards lost by Crassus at Carrhae; cosmic register with Apollo, Aurora, Diana, Earth). Based on Polykleitos' Doryphoros pose. The most famous portrait type of Augustus. |
| **Roman copy of the Doryphoros (Spear-Bearer)** | Braccio Nuovo | Roman marble copy of Polykleitos' canonical 5th-century BCE Greek bronze. |
| **Statue of the Nile** (Nilo) | Braccio Nuovo | Roman river-god reclining with the Nile's tributaries; crocodile at his feet. |
| **Gilded Bronze Peacocks** (Pavoni) | Braccio Nuovo | Two splendid gilt-bronze peacocks; originals from Hadrian's tomb (Castel Sant'Angelo) are nearby — the Cortile della Pigna peacocks are copies. |
| **Canova's fresco cycle** | Chiaramonti loggia lunettes | Young artists' frescoes commemorating the papal patronage and the return of the Vatican works from France (panel XXI). |

## 5. MUSEO GREGORIANO ETRUSCO (Gregorian Etruscan Museum)

Founded by Gregory XVI; inaugurated 2 February 1837 — one of the first museums dedicated to Etruscan antiquities. 22 rooms (>2,000 m²) in the Palazzetto del Belvedere (Innocent VIII, designed by Pollaiolo) and the Tor dei Venti apartment (Pius IV). ~15,000 inventoried works, ~4,000 on display. Original 16th-century frescoes by Federico Barocci, Federico Zuccari, Santi di Tito, Niccolò Circignani.

### Key rooms and artifacts
| Room / Area | Highlights | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Room II — Regolini-Galassi Tomb** | **Regolini-Galassi Tomb assemblage** | Tomb discovered intact in 1836 at Cerveteri; orientalising period (c. 675–650 BCE). Jewels (pectoral, large fibula, bracelets, necklace), silver vessels (oinochoe, hemispherical cup, patera, skyphos, amphora), bronze funerary bed, three restored carts, shields, "Phoenician-Cypriot" patera. Objects inscribed *larthia* ("I belong to Larth"). |
| **Goldsmith rooms** | Etruscan gold jewelry | Refined pieces from Cerveteri and Tarquinia demonstrating Etruscan goldwork mastery. |
| **Mars of Todi** | **Mars of Todi** (Marte di Todi) | Bronze statuette of a warrior (Mars), early 4th century BCE; a masterpiece of Etruscan bronze work. |
| **Vase Collection (Rooms XVIII–XXII)** | Greek and Italiot vases | Attic black-figure amphora signed by **Exekias**; Attic kylix attributed to **Douris** (Jason); Attic hydria by the **Berlin Painter**; Eponymous amphora of the **Achilles Painter**; krater by the **Painter of the Boston Phiale**; late Corinthian krater. A history of ancient Greek painting from Etruscan graves (Vulci, Cerveteri). |
| **Sarcophagi** | Sarcophagus with polychrome relief; **Cinerary Urn of the Master of Oenomaus**; Funerary monument with Dying Adonis | Important Etruscan terracotta and stone funerary art. |

| **Antiquarium Romanum** | Roman bronzes, glass, ivory, terracotta | Extends to the end of the Western Empire (5th century CE). |

## 6. MUSEO GREGORIANO EGIZIO (Gregorian Egyptian Museum)

Founded 1839 by Gregory XVI; curated by Barnabite Fr. Luigi Ungarelli (disciple of Ippolito Rosellini). Nine rooms + hemicycle + Terrace of the Niche, in the former apartment of Pius IV in the Palace of the Belvedere. The collection is distinguished by its *territorial identity*: most objects were already in Rome since antiquity — from the Gardens of Sallust, from Hadrian's Villa at Tivoli, and from Roman Egypt. Last three rooms: Ancient Near East (Mesopotamia, Syria-Palestine).

### Room-by-room Highlights
| Room | Highlights | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Room I — Epigraphic Artefacts** | Fragmented statue of **Ramesses II enthroned**; two Old Kingdom false-door funerary stelae; Saite Renaissance limestone relief (from Theban Tomb 34 of Mentuemhat); commemorative stele of **Queen Hatshepsut and Thutmose III**; **Naophorus statue of Udjahorresnet** (Persian conquest period, 525 BCE) — the most important historical document in the collection; Christian Egyptian inscriptions. | Hieroglyphic and figured remains from 2600 BCE–600 CE. |
| **Room II — Funerary Customs** | Two **mummies**; polychrome wooden sarcophagi of the Third Intermediate Period ("yellow coffins" — among the oldest wood paintings in history); grave-goods reconstruction; **Shroud of the "Lady of the Vatican"** (Roman-era linen from Antinopolis); Fayum mummy portrait of a young man; **Sarcophagus of Djedmut** (singer of Amon-Ra at Karnak, early Dynasty XXII, c. 945–900 BCE; painted wood, h. 209 cm). | Burial customs; the "house of eternity" concept. |
| **Room III** | Statues from **Hadrian's Villa, Tivoli** (black basalt, imitating Egyptian originals) | Roman-era Egyptian material. |
| **Room V / Hemicycle** | Sculptures from the **Gardens of Sallust (Horti Sallustiani)**; **statue of Osiris-Antinous**; **Statue of Queen Tuya** (mother of Ramesses II); group with **Ptolemy II**; **Palmyran burial reliefs**; **Lions of Nectanebo**; colossal Roman **statue of the Nile** (1st–2nd c. CE, crocodile at feet) | Opens onto the Terrace of the Niche. |
| **Terrace of the Niche (Nicchione)** | Three Dynasty XXVI sarcophagi from Memphis; **eight statues of the lioness goddess Sekhmet** from the Temple Precinct of Mut at Karnak (plus four more in the hemicycle); colossal **bronze Pinecone** dominating the courtyard (1st–2nd c. CE Roman fountain; signed by Publius Cincius Salvius; once in the atrium of Old St. Peter's — cited by Dante, Inferno XXXI; moved here 1608); two **reclining lions of Nectanebo II** at the fountain sides | Terrace built 1562 by Pirro Ligorio. |
| **Book of the Dead** | Papyrus (~30 pages of funerary formulas) | Reproduction/original of the famous Egyptian funerary text. |
| **Rooms VII–IX — Ancient Near East** | Mesopotamian and Syrian-Palestine artifacts | Added to the collection in the 1970s. |
| **Carlo Grassi Collection** | Egyptian bronzes | Part of the Egyptian Museum collections. |

**Research projects:** Vatican Mummy Project (DNA of mummies); Vatican Coffin Project (wood-painting technique bridging ancient Egypt and Giotto); Sekhmet Project (lioness statues from Amenhotep III's funerary temple, Thebes).

## 7. PINACOTECA VATICANA (Vatican Picture Gallery)

Inaugurated 27 October 1932 in a building purpose-built by architect Luca Beltrami for Pius XI, in the Square Garden (Giardino Quadrato). ~460 paintings in 18 chronological rooms, from the "Primitives" (12th–13th c.) to the 19th century. A first nucleus of 118 paintings was created by Pius VI c. 1790; works lost to Napoleon were returned after 1815.

## 8. SISTINE CHAPEL (Cappella Sistina)

Named for Pope Sixtus IV della Rovere, who restored the Cappella Magna 1477–1480. Consecrated 15 August 1483 to Our Lady of the Assumption. Site of the Papal Conclave. Dimensions ~40.9 m long × 13.4 m wide × 20.7 m high. Wall frescoes 1481–1482; ceiling by Michelangelo 1508–1512; Last Judgment 1536–1541. Full restoration 1979–1999.

### 8.1 Ceiling — Michelangelo (1508–1512)
~930 m², 300+ figures. Painted in four years; inaugurated 31 October/1 November 1512. Julius II had asked for twelve apostles; Michelangelo proposed the full Genesis program.

**Nine central panels (Genesis scenes), from the altar toward the entrance:**
| # | Scene | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Separation of Light from Darkness** | God divides light from the void; the final narrative painted. |
| 2 | **The Creation of the Sun, Moon and Plants** | God creates the luminaries and vegetation. |
| 3 | **The Separation of Land and Water** | God divides sea from dry land. |
| 4 | **The Creation of Adam** | The iconic near-touching hands of God and Adam; Eve watches from beneath God's cloak. |
| 5 | **The Creation of Eve** | Eve emerges from Adam's side, hands raised in reverence. |
| 6 | **The Fall and Expulsion from the Garden of Eden** | Temptation by the serpent and the expulsion combined in one panel. |
| 7 | **The Sacrifice of Noah** | Noah offers thanksgiving sacrifice after the Flood. |
| 8 | **The Great Flood** | Humanity perishes; the ark floats in the background. |
| 9 | **The Drunkenness of Noah** | Noah drunk, mocked by his sons — human weakness in the chosen patriarch. |

**Twelve Prophets and Sibyls (seated on thrones in the spandrels):**
Jonah (above the altar), Jeremiah, Persian Sibyl, Ezekiel, Erythraean Sibyl, Joel, Zechariah (above the entrance door), Delphic Sibyl, Isaiah, Cumaean Sibyl, Daniel, Libyan Sibyl.

**Four corner pendentives (salvation of Israel):**
- **The Brazen Serpent** (Moses)
- **The Crucifixion of Haman (Esther)**
- **David and Goliath**
- **Judith and Holofernes**

**Ignudi:** 20 athletic nude youths framing the five smaller central scenes, each pair enclosing a medallion. Likely represent the perfection of humanity or angels; a pattern book for later artists.

**Ten medallions (bronze-colored shields, violent episodes from the Kings of Israel):** Abraham and Isaac; Destruction of the Statue of Baal; slaughter of Baal's worshippers; Death of Uriah; Nathan condemning David; Absalom caught by his hair; Joab murdering Abner; Joram hurled from his chariot; Elijah carried to Heaven.

**Ancestors of Christ:** 14 lunettes above the windows + 8 spandrels above them, depicting 40 generations from Abraham to Jesse/David. Named groups include Jesse/David/Solomon; Josiah/Jechoniah/Shealtiel; Hezekiah/Manasseh/Amon; Asa/Jehosaphat/Joram; Uzziah/Jotham/Ahaz; Zerubbabel/Abiud/Eliakim; Eleazar/Mathan; Salmon/Boaz/Obed; Rehoboam/Abijah; Aminadab; Naason; Azor/Sadoc; Jacob/Joseph; Achim/Eliud.

### 8.3 The Last Judgment (Altar Wall) — Michelangelo (1536–1541)
- ~200 × 10 m fresco; 300+ figures; commissioned by Clement VII, executed under Paul III.
- Christ in glory at the center (a more dynamic, Mannerist figure than the ceiling's Creation panels), surrounded by Mary, apostles, martyrs, confessors.
- The saved rise on the left; the damned are dragged to hell on the right.
- **Charon** (from Dante's Inferno) ferries souls; **Minos** judges the damned (with donkey ears).
- Michelangelo painted **his own face** onto the flayed skin held by St. Bartholomew (lower right).
- Below: seven cardinal virtues/angels with instruments of the Passion; at the very bottom, the resurrection of the dead from their graves.

### 8.4 Marble elements (1482–1483)
- **Choir screen** (transenna) with the papal coat of arms of Sixtus IV over the entrance.
- **Choir stalls** for the choristers.
- The **cantoria** (singers' gallery).

### 8.5 Sistine Chapel Rules
- Absolute silence enforced ("silenzio" called out by guards).
- Photography strictly forbidden (all commercial images are licensed).
- It is a consecrated church; appropriate dress required.

## 9. RAPHAEL ROOMS (Stanze di Raffaello)

Four rooms in the Apostolic Palace frescoed by Raphael and his workshop for Julius II and Leo X. Painted in the visitor's order: **Sala di Costantino** (largest, painted entirely after Raphael's death), **Stanza di Eliodoro**, **Stanza della Segnatura** (first painted), **Stanza dell'Incendio del Borgo**. Chronological order of execution: Segnatura → Eliodoro → Incendio → Costantino. Dimensions of the wall frescoes: ~500 × 770 cm (Segnatura) and ~750 cm wide (Eliodoro).

### 9.1 Stanza della Segnatura (Room of the Signatura), 1508–1511
Julius II's private library and the chamber of the Apostolic Signatura (where papal documents were sealed). The program celebrates the harmony of classical philosophy and Christian theology through the four branches of human knowledge.
| Wall | Fresco | Description |
|---|---|---|
| West | **The Disputation of the Holy Sacrament** (La Disputa, 1509–1510, 500 × 770 cm) | Heaven and earth gather around the Eucharist: the Trinity above, Mary, John the Baptist, martyrs, prophets; below, the Church Militant — saints, doctors, and scholars in animated discussion around the monstrance. A theology of the Eucharist in pictorial form. |
| East | **The School of Athens** (1509–1511, 500 × 770 cm) | The most famous fresco of the High Renaissance: ancient philosophers gathered in a grand imaginary classical architecture. Plato (pointing upward, modeled on Leonardo da Vinci) and Aristotle (pointing earthward, holding the Ethics) at the center; Pythagoras, Euclid, Heraclitus (portrait of Michelangelo, brooding on the steps), Ptolemy, Zoroaster. Raphael painted himself into the group (far right, beside Ptolemy, wearing a black beret). |
| North | **The Parnassus** (Parnaso, 1509–1511) | Apollo playing the lyre on Mount Parnassus, surrounded by the nine Muses and poets (Homer, Virgil, Dante, Petrarch) — the domain of poetry and inspiration. |
| South | **Cardinal and Theological Virtues** (1509–1511) | Allegorical female figures of Fortitude, Prudence, Temperance (cardinal) and Faith, Hope, Charity (theological), with Justice above. |
| Ceiling | Tondi of **Theology, Philosophy, Poetry, Jurisprudence** with scenes of Adam and Eve, The Judgment of Solomon, Urania, The Flaying of Marsyas | The four branches of knowledge as personified in medallions. |

### 9.2 Stanza di Eliodoro (Room of Heliodorus), 1511–1514
A private audience room for Julius II. Theme: heavenly protection granted to Christ's Church.
| Wall | Fresco | Description |
|---|---|---|
| West | **The Expulsion of Heliodorus from the Temple** (1511–1512) | God's intervention to protect the Temple of Jerusalem: angels drive out the profane Heliodorus, watched from the left by Julius II (carried in by Swiss Guard). Dynamic, theatrical composition. |
| East | **The Mass at Bolsena** (1512) | The 1263 miracle of the bleeding Host at Bolsena, confirming the doctrine of Transubstantiation for a doubting Bohemian priest. Julius II kneeling at the altar (lower right). |
| North | **The Liberation of Saint Peter from Prison** (1513–1514) | Three scenes in one: the sleeping Peter, the angel freeing him, Peter led past sleeping guards. Dramatic use of light (moon, angelic glow). |
| South | **The Meeting of Pope Leo the Great and Attila** (1513–1514) | Pope Leo I turns back Attila the Hun in 452, with Sts. Peter and Paul appearing in the sky. Portrait of Julius II's successor, Leo X, as the cardinal in the procession. |
| Ceiling | Scenes of God's protection of the Church (including The Burning Bush, Jacob's Ladder, The Promise to Abraham, Noah's Sacrifice) | theological prefiguration of the room's theme. |

### 9.3 Stanza dell'Incendio del Borgo (Room of the Fire in the Borgo), 1514–1517
Named for its principal fresco. Prepared as a music room for Leo X. Executed mainly by Raphael's workshop (Giulio Romano) after Raphael's mature designs.
| Wall | Fresco | Description |
|---|---|---|
| West | **The Fire in the Borgo** (1514–1517) | Pope Leo IV extinguishes the 847 fire in the Borgo with the sign of the cross from the Loggia of Blessings. Raphael's design, executed by Giulio Romano. |
| East | **The Battle of Ostia** | Leo IV's victory over the Saracens at Ostia, 849. |
| South | **The Coronation of Charlemagne** (1516–1517) | Leo III crowns Charlemagne Emperor on Christmas Day, 800. |
| North | **The Oath of Leo III** | Leo III purging himself of false accusations before Charlemagne, 800. |

### 9.4 Sala di Costantino (Hall of Constantine), 1517–1524
The largest room; frescoed entirely by Raphael's workshop (Giulico Romano, Gianfrancesco Penni) after his death, completed under Clement VII. Theme: the triumph of Christianity over paganism through Constantine.
| Wall | Fresco | Description |
|---|---|---|
| North | **The Vision of the Cross** | Constantine sees the cross with the sign "In hoc signo vinces" before the Battle of the Milvian Bridge. |
| West | **The Battle of the Milvian Bridge** (1520–1524) | Constantine's victory over Maxentius, 312 CE. |
| East | **The Baptism of Constantine** | Constantine baptized by Pope Sylvester in the Lateran (actually by Pope Sylvester — the legendary account). |
| South | **The Donation of Constantine** (1520–1524) | The forged "Donation" by which Constantine supposedly granted Rome and the West to the Pope — a key text of papal territorial claims. |
| Ceiling | Allegories of Christian virtues | Completed by the workshop. |

## 10. BORGIA APARTMENT (Appartamento Borgia) & COLLECTION OF MODERN AND CONTEMPORARY ART

### 10.1 Borgia Apartment — Pinturicchio, 1492–1494
Pope Alexander VI (Rodrigo Borgia, 1492–1503) commissioned Bernardino di Betto (Pinturicchio) to decorate his private apartments. Six monumental rooms. Lavish stucco, gilded panels, fictive tapestries, and esoteric iconography inspired by the humanist Annio da Viterbo. Now houses the Collection of Modern and Contemporary Art.

| Room | Fresco program | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Room of the Pontiffs** (Sala dei Pontefici) | Papal portraits in lunettes | The oldest wing (Nicholas III, 1277–1280); the ceremonial entrance hall. |
| **Room of the Liberal Arts** (Sala delle Arti Liberali) | Grammar, Rhetoric, Logic (Trivium); Geometry, Arithmetic, Music, Astronomy (Quadrivium) | Celestial women personifying the liberal arts with their patrons (Cicero with Rhetoric, Euclid with Geometry); walls with biblical episodes (Jacob fleeing Laban, Trajan and the Poor Widow); ceiling with Borgia heraldry (bull, double crown). |
| **Room of the Saints** (Sala dei Santi) | St. Elizabeth (Visitation), St. Anthony Abbot & St. Paul the Hermit, St. Catherine's Disputation, St. Barbara, St. Susanna, St. Sebastian; ceiling: the Isis-Osiris myth and the myth of Io from Ovid | Recognized as a Pinturicchio masterpiece; tondo of the Madonna and Child said to portray Giulia Farnese. |
| **Room of the Mysteries** (Sala dei Misteri) | Annunciation, Nativity, Adoration of the Magi, Resurrection, Ascension, Pentecost, Assumption | Alexander VI kneeling at Christ's feet in the Resurrection. The vault cross with 8 prophet medallions; restoration (2006) recovered original colors and revealed the grotesques. ## 11. THE THREE GREAT GALLERIES (Corridors)

### 11.1 Galleria dei Candelabri (Gallery of the Candelabra)
~80 m corridor, six sections divided by pairs of massive ancient marble candelabra. Part of the Pio-Clementino Museum. Built as an open loggia in 1761 by Pius VI; walled in and rearranged 1785–1788; completely renovated under Leo XIII (1883–1887) by Annibale Angelini with frescoes by Domenico Torti (The Triumph of Truth) and **Ludwig Seitz** (grand ceiling series honoring St. Thomas Aquinas). Floor: marble inlays with ancient mosaic fragments (some from Otricoli); center features Leo XIII's coat of arms with lapis lazuli.

| Section | Highlights |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Gigantic toe of a right foot** — from a ~13 m statue near the Colosseum (possibly Venus and Rome temple). |
| 2 | **Meridian** from Ostia (1858); **Artemis of Ephesus** — fertility goddess with multiple breasts/bull testicles. |
| 3 | Second-century still-life mosaic from Tor Marancia; **Hypnos (God of Sleep)** and **Thanatos (Genius of Death)** (mid-2nd c., after Praxiteles, from Villa of Cassius, Tivoli, 1774). |
| 4 | **Statue of an Old Fisherman** (2nd c., from Anzio, 1773) — striking realism; **Dionysus on a satyr** (from Scala Santa, 1854); **Boy Strangling a Goose** (copy after Boethus, 2nd c. BCE, from Villa Quintili, 1789). |
| 5 | **Circular Altar** (1st–2nd c. BCE, Tor Colombaro, Appian Way) — sacrifice scene with Mercury. |
| 6 | **The Persian Warrior** (Roman copy, c. 110 CE, of a votive group of Attalos II of Pergamon; found 1503–1512 at Palazzo Medici/Madama); Dionysus/satyr. |

### 11.2 Galleria degli Arazzi (Gallery of the Tapestries)
~75 m corridor (245 ft). Ceiling decorated by Bernardino Nocchi (1788–1789) under Pius VI: scenes celebrating the virtues of Pius VI foreshadowed in Roman emperors (Nerva, Trajan, Alexander Severus; Clemency of Titus; Antoninus Pius vs. Numa Pompilius).

**Tapestries:**
- **Left wall (Raphael tapestries):** Designed by Raphael and woven in Brussels (Pieter van Aelst workshop, c. 1516–1521) for the Sistine Chapel, commissioned by Clement VII. Raphael's "cartoons" were drawn bottom-to-top (for low-warp looms). Subjects include Christ's charge to Peter, the stoning of St. Stephen, the conversion of St. Paul, etc.
- **Right wall:** Ancient pontifical tapestries from the collections (Flemish and Italian workmanship).
- The gallery is also famous for its **trompe-l'oeil floor borders**.

### 11.3 Galleria delle Carte Geografiche (Gallery of Maps)
120 m long × 6 m wide. Built 1578–1580 by Ottaviano Nonni ("Mascherino") for Gregory XIII. Frescoed in under two years by a team coordinated by Girolamo Muziano and Cesare Nebbia from a program by **Egnazio Danti** (Perugian Dominican cosmographer, mathematician, and astronomer). Restored 2005–2010.

**The maps (40 total):**
- Two entrance maps: **Italia Antiqua** and **Italia Nova**.
- **32 regional maps** of the peninsula arranged north→south along the Apennine spine; each ~300 × 400 cm; Tyrrhenian/Ligurian-side regions on the Belvedere wall, Adriatic/Alps-side regions on the Gardens wall. Regions include: Piedmont, Liguria, Lombardy, Veneto, Emilia-Romagna, Tuscany, Umbria, Marche, Lazio, Abruzzo, Campania, Puglia, Basilicata, Calabria, Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, and others.
- **8 narrow maps at the ends:** Siege of Malta (1565), Battle of Lepanto (1571), Elba, Tremiti Islands; and the four ports: Civitavecchia, Genoa, Venice, Ancona.

**Ceiling:** 51 panels in rich gilded stucco frames; each depicts a scene from Church history, the Old Testament, or the lives of saints associated with the region below (e.g., Battle of Metauro, siege of Mirandola). Anamorphosis of the Eucharist once at the north end.

**Significance:** The world's largest pictorial geographical study; the first time Italy — then politically fragmented — was represented as a geographical, historical, and cultural unity.

## 12. ARCHITECTURAL FEATURES

### 12.1 Bramante Staircase (Scala del Momo / "Bramante Staircase")
- **Modern staircase (1929–1932):** Designed by **Giuseppe Momo** (1875–1940), architect of the Reverenda Fabbrica di San Pietro; sculpted by Antonio Maraini; realized by the Ferdinando Marinelli Artistic Foundry. A **double helix** — two separate ramps so ascending and descending visitors never cross. Encircles a ~15 m diameter stairwell with a clear central void. Shallow incline (stepped ramp, not a true staircase); ornately worked metal balustrade; conical geometry with reverse-truncated-cone form. All visitors exit via this route. Often compared to Frank Lloyd Wright's Guggenheim (20 years earlier).
- **Original Bramante Staircase (1505):** In the Pio-Clementino Museum, a true double spiral in a square tower by Donato Bramante.
- Location: at the end of the visitor route, near the exit.

### 12.2 Cortile della Pigna (Pinecone Courtyard)
- A ~300 m² open-air courtyard at the start of the visit, part of the original Cortile del Belvedere (designed by Donato Bramante, 1504–1514, for Julius II, to connect the Palazzetto di Innocent VIII with the Sistine Chapel).
- **Fontana della Pigna (Pinecone Fountain):** Colossal ancient Roman **bronze pinecone**, nearly 4 m high and 2.5 m wide, made in the 1st–2nd century CE (signed by Publius Cincius Salvius; lost-wax cast in five sections). Originally a fountain near the Pantheon (by the Temple of Isis), later in the atrium (cantharos Paradisi) of Old St. Peter's — cited by Dante (Inferno XXXI). Moved to the Belvedere in 1608. Stands on a monumental marble capital from Nero's Baths (found under Alexander VII, Chi) decorated with gym games reliefs; installed by architect Francesco Fontana in 1704 for Clement XI.
- **Bronze peacocks** flanking the fountain — copies of 2nd-century CE originals from Hadrian's tomb (in the Braccio Nuovo).
- **Arnaldo Pomodoro's Sphere Within Sphere (see 12.3).**

### 12.3 Sphere Within Sphere (Sfera con Sfera) — Arnaldo Pomodoro
- Donated by the artist in 1990; installed in the Cortile della Pigna.
- **Date:** 1990 (created 1979–1980). **Medium:** polished bronze. **Dimensions:** diameter 4 m (same size as the golden ball atop the Dome of St. Peter's — a deliberate reference). **Weight:** ~4 tons.
- A large sphere fissured to reveal a complex inner world of gears and mechanical forms; can rotate on a ball-bearing mechanism (also turned by wind).
- Metaphor: the tension between exterior perfection and interior complexity; "spirit and matter"; a "wounded planet" reflecting sky and crowds.
- Sister spheres exist worldwide (UN Headquarters, Trinity College Dublin, etc.); Pomodoro made ~45 in total. The Vatican's is the most famous.

### 12.4 Cortile del Belvedere (Belvedere Courtyard)
- Designed by **Donato Bramante** (1504–1514) for Julius II to connect the Palace of Innocent VIII with the Sistine Chapel; completed after Bramante's death by **Pirro Ligorio**, who added the great niche (1565, modeled on the Pantheon's apse) at the north end.
- Originally divided into three terraces with ramps; pavement sloped toward the Sistine Chapel for perspectival effect. Later divided by the construction of the Vatican Library; the upper part became the **Cortile della Pigna**, the lower the **Cortile delle Corazze** (or Cortile del Belvedere proper). Orientation points on the official map: CC = Cortile delle Corazze; 4C = Quattro Cancelli; CP = Cortile della Pigna; Q = Giardino Quadrato.
- The Nicchione (great niche) of the Cortile della Pigna closes the perspective from the papal apartments.

### 12.5 Other architectural features
- **Scala Simonetti** — monumental ramp (1771–1784) leading into the Gregorian Egyptian Museum.
- **Atrium of the Four Gates (Quattro Cancelli) / Atrium of the Corazze** — orientation courts near the entrance.
- **Sala Regia** — ceremonial hall decorated by Giorgio Vasari (victories of the papacy, Battle of Lepanto), adjacent to the Sistine Chapel.
- **Giardino Quadrato (Square Garden)** — the 19th-century garden where the Pinacoteca building stands, surrounded by avenues.
- **Cappella Niccolina** — decorated by Fra Angelico (1447–1449) for Nicholas V; not always open to the general route.
